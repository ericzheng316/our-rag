\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\label{sec:starvation}

\subsection{Setting and notation}
\label{sec:starve-setting}

A rollout is a trajectory
$\tau=(s_1,a_1,o_1,\ldots,s_T,a_T)$ in which actions are protocol steps:
a plan, search queries, and a terminal answer (or an abstention); each
search returns retrieved paragraphs as the observation. During training
the environment is a closed candidate pool per question; gold supporting
documents $\mathcal{G}$ are privileged training annotations and play no
role at test time (\S\ref{sec:setup}). Outcome-supervised systems assign
each rollout a return of the form
\begin{equation}
R(\tau) \;=\; R_{\mathrm{ans}}(\tau)
\;-\; \lambda_{s} N_{\mathrm{search}}(\tau) \;-\; P_{\mathrm{aux}}(\tau),
\label{eq:base-reward}
\end{equation}
where $R_{\mathrm{ans}}$ is the terminal answer reward (alias-aware exact
match against the gold answer set), $N_{\mathrm{search}}$ counts search
calls, and $P_{\mathrm{aux}}$ collects format, turn-limit, and free-token
penalties; \emph{free tokens} are the tokens a rollout emits outside
protocol actions.

\subsection{Group-relative learning and answer-tied groups}
\label{sec:starve-ties}

Group-relative estimators draw $K$ rollouts of the same question and
compare them against each other. With a leave-one-out
baseline~\citep{kool2019buy,ahmadian2024rloo}, the advantage of rollout
$i$ is
\begin{equation}
A_i \;=\; R_i \;-\; \frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{j\neq i} R_j ,
\label{eq:rloo}
\end{equation}
broadcast to the tokens of every turn of $\tau_i$, so any reward
component that is constant within the group cancels exactly. We call a
group \emph{answer-tied} when all $K$ rollouts receive the same answer
reward---every rollout wrong or every rollout right. In an answer-tied
group, $R_{\mathrm{ans}}$ is such a constant: it cancels from every
advantage in Eq.~\ref{eq:rloo}, and the group yields no gradient from
answer correctness, however differently its rollouts behaved.

How often does this happen? We sample $K{=}8$ rollouts per question at
the training temperature from the SFT initialization on a stratified
question set (protocol in \S\ref{sec:mechanism}).
Table~\ref{tab:tie-ledger} shows the result: ties are the dominant
regime, 56--71\% of groups at every depth. Depth changes which kind of
tie dominates. At two hops, 28.1\% of groups are tied because every
rollout is right. At four hops that share is 4.4\%, and 57.4\% of all
groups are tied because every rollout is wrong. The deeper the
question, the less the terminal reward can say precisely where there
is the most left to learn.

\subsection{The residual ordering prefers cheap failure}
\label{sec:starve-cost}

What ranks the rollouts of an all-failure group under
Eq.~\ref{eq:base-reward}? Substituting into Eq.~\ref{eq:rloo} with
$R_{\mathrm{ans}}$ constant,
\begin{equation}
A_i \;=\; -\lambda_{s}\bigl(N_i - \bar{N}_{-i}\bigr)
\;-\;\bigl(P_i - \bar{P}_{-i}\bigr),
\label{eq:cost-ordering}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{N}_{-i}$, $\bar{P}_{-i}$ are leave-one-out means. The
surviving preference is purely economic: the rollout that searched
less, generated less, and gave up sooner outranks the one that kept
pursuing evidence---even if the latter recovered most of the required
chain and the former recovered none. On the questions where persistence
is the very skill to be learned, the effective training signal points
the other way. Empirically, replaying the outcome-only reward over the
all-failure groups of the $K{=}8$ audit (\S\ref{sec:mechanism})
measures both properties: the within-group advantage ranks rollouts
almost purely by how little they searched (mean within-group Spearman
$\rho = -0.91$ against search count, negative in 97\% of groups) and
only weakly by evidence coverage ($\rho = +0.13$).
The ordering that survives punishes persistence and ignores progress
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism}B).\footnote{Eq.~\ref{eq:rloo} applies no
group standard-deviation normalization; \citet{wang2026darkroom}
analyze the $z$-scored case.}

\subsection{Coverage restores an ordering}
\label{sec:starve-fix}

Let $C_i \in [0,1]$ be the fraction of the gold evidence set that
rollout $i$ retrieves (Eq.~\ref{eq:coverage} below). Adding
$\beta\,C$ to the return contributes
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
A^{\mathrm{cov}}_i \;&=\; \beta\Bigl(C_i - \frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{j\neq i}
C_j\Bigr) \\
&=\; \beta\,\frac{K}{K-1}\bigl(C_i - \bar{C}\bigr)
\end{split}
\label{eq:cov-advantage}
\end{equation}
to the advantage. The group mean $\bar{C}$ enters every rollout of
the group identically, so only each rollout's deviation from it
survives: the term ranks the rollouts of a group by coverage. At the
SFT initialization, rollouts differ in coverage in 31.0\% of
answer-tied groups at two hops, 70.4\% at three, and 84.3\% at four,
so the term orders almost every deep tied group that the answer reward
leaves unordered. Counting a group as trainable when either signal
discriminates, coverage expands the informative share of 4-hop groups
from 38.2\% to 88.7\% ($\times$2.3).

\subsection{Evidence gain of a query}
\label{sec:method-gain}

Let $\mathcal{G}$ be the question's annotated set of gold supporting
documents, matched by document title, and let $D_t$ be the documents
retrieved by the query $q_t$ issued at search turn $t$. The
\emph{evidence gain} of $q_t$ is the share of $\mathcal{G}$ that the
rollout reaches for the first time at turn $t$:
\begin{equation}
g(q_t) \;=\; \frac{\bigl|\,(D_t \cap \mathcal{G}) \setminus
\textstyle\bigcup_{t'<t} D_{t'}\,\bigr|}{|\mathcal{G}|}.
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
Each gold document is credited once, at its first retrieval;
re-retrieving it earns nothing. The \emph{coverage} of a rollout,
\begin{equation}
C(\tau) \;=\; \sum_t g(q_t) \;=\; \frac{\bigl|\{\, d \in \mathcal{G} :
\tau \text{ retrieves } d \,\}\bigr|}{|\mathcal{G}|},
\label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
is the fraction of $\mathcal{G}$ it reaches over the whole trajectory.

\subsection{Query-level credit}
\label{sec:method-credit}

At each search turn $t$ of rollout $\tau_i$, let $s_t$ be the state---the
context preceding the query. We sample $M$ additional queries
$q'_1,\ldots,q'_M \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid s_t)$, retrieve for each
against the same environment, and compute their evidence gains under
Eq.~\ref{eq:gain} with the same prior-hit set. The credit of the
executed query is
\begin{equation}
c_{i,t} \;=\; \beta_q \Bigl( g(q_t) - \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}
g(q'_m) \Bigr).
\label{eq:credit}
\end{equation}
The $M$ samples enter Eq.~\ref{eq:credit} only; the trajectory
continues with $q_t$. The credit is added to the advantage of the
tokens of turn $t$,
\begin{equation}
A_{i,t} \;=\; A_i + c_{i,t},
\label{eq:turn-adv}
\end{equation}
and answer turns keep $A_i$. Retrieval uses the first 16 words of a
query; tokens beyond the cap do not reach the retriever, so a query
cannot raise its credit by appending terms. Values of $M$ and $\beta_q$
are given in Appendix~\ref{app:procedure}.

\subsection{What the credit estimates}
\label{sec:method-estimate}

Let $r_g(s,q)=g(q)$ be the one-step evidence gain of issuing query $q$
at state $s$, and $V^\pi_g(s)=\mathbb{E}_{q\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid
s)}[g(q)]$ its expectation under the current policy. The sample mean in
Eq.~\ref{eq:credit} is a Monte-Carlo estimate of $V^\pi_g(s_t)$ from
samples drawn at the visited state, as in the vine estimator of
\citet{schulman2015trpo}, so $c_{i,t}$ estimates $\beta_q\,A^\pi_g(s_t,q_t)$
with $A^\pi_g(s,q)=r_g(s,q)-V^\pi_g(s)$: the leave-one-out contrast of
Eq.~\ref{eq:rloo}, applied at the search state to $M$ samples of the
same policy instead of at the question to the $K$ rollouts. Over the
executed query the estimate is zero-mean,
$\mathbb{E}_{q_t\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s_t)}[c_{i,t}]=0$, so the credit
leaves the expected return of every trajectory unchanged. Its
contribution to the policy gradient is
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[c_{i,t}\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(q_t\mid s_t)\bigr]
\;=\;\beta_q\,\nabla_\theta V^\pi_g(s_t),
\label{eq:credit-grad}
\end{equation}
the gradient of the expected evidence gain at the visited state: at
each search state the update moves probability from queries that reach
no new gold evidence to queries that do. Summed along a rollout,
\begin{equation}
\sum_t c_{i,t} \;=\; \beta_q\Bigl(C(\tau_i)-\sum_t \hat V_g(s_{i,t})\Bigr),
\label{eq:credit-sum}
\end{equation}
coverage relative to the policy's expected coverage along the same
path, assigned to the turns that produced it; the trajectory-level term
of Eq.~\ref{eq:cov-advantage} contributes
$\beta\tfrac{K}{K-1}(C_i-\bar C)$, coverage relative to the other
rollouts of the question, assigned to every turn alike
(Appendix~\ref{app:traj}). The credit is non-zero at a state whenever
$\mathrm{Var}_{q\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s)}[g(q)]>0$, which holds at
35--50\% of the search states of the trained policy in the closed pool
(\S\ref{sec:mech-broadcast}). In an answer-tied group the trajectory
advantage $A_i$ carries only the cost terms of
Eq.~\ref{eq:cost-ordering}, and the credit is the signal that orders
the query.
