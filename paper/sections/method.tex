\section{Coverage-Augmented Trajectory Ranking}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Reward definition}
\label{sec:method-reward}

Each rollout $\tau$ receives a scalar trajectory return
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
R(\tau) \;=\;{}& R_{\mathrm{ans}}(\tau)
\;+\; \mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathrm{ans}(\tau)\right]\, \beta\, C(\tau) \\
&\;-\; \lambda_{s}\, N_{\mathrm{search}}(\tau)
\;-\; P_{\mathrm{aux}}(\tau),
\end{split}
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
where $R_{\mathrm{ans}}$ is the terminal answer reward (alias-aware exact
match against the gold answer set), $N_{\mathrm{search}}$ counts search
calls, and $P_{\mathrm{aux}}$ collects format, turn-limit, and free-token
penalties. The new term is the \emph{evidence-coverage} fraction
\begin{equation}
C(\tau) \;=\; \frac{\bigl|\{\, d \in \mathcal{G} :
\tau \text{ retrieves } d \,\}\bigr|}{|\mathcal{G}|},
\label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{G}$ is the question's annotated set of gold supporting
documents, matched by document title. Each gold document is credited at
most once, at its first retrieval (\emph{first-hit}); re-retrieving an
already-credited document earns nothing, so the term rewards evidence
\emph{progress}, not retrieval volume.

Two design choices matter. \textbf{Completion gating}: the coverage
credit is paid only if the rollout commits to an answer. Without the
gate, a policy can farm evidence credit while abstaining---in early
ungated runs we observed abstention rates rising alongside coverage
reward, the expected signature of this arbitrage. Gating ties the
process credit to the behavior the task actually demands.
\textbf{Determinism}: we compute coverage as the set overlap between
retrieved evidence and the training annotations; the signal is
deterministic and used only during training.

\subsection{The term is a trajectory-ranking signal}
\label{sec:method-effect}

Coverage credit is emitted incrementally, at the turn where each first
hit occurs. With discounting near one, its trajectory sum equals
$\beta\,C(\tau)$ up to negligible correction, and the estimator computes
a single leave-one-out advantage per trajectory
(\S\ref{sec:starvation})---so the term acts on \emph{trajectory returns
and their within-group ranking}. This is a different mechanism from
step-level process supervision~\citep{lightman2024verify,
uesato2022solving}, and a simpler one: no per-turn credit model is
involved anywhere. Following the
decomposition in \S\ref{sec:starvation}, the term's contribution to the
relative advantage of rollout $i$ is
$\beta\,\tfrac{K}{K-1}\bigl(C_i - \bar{C}\bigr)$: a common coverage
offset cancels, and what remains is exactly the within-group ordering
that terminal correctness cannot provide in an answer-tied group.
Uniformly failing rollouts are thereby ranked by how much of the
required evidence they reached, displacing the cost-driven ordering of
\S\ref{sec:starvation}.

\subsection{Stabilizing long-horizon optimization}
\label{sec:method-kl}

Trajectories mix two token populations: protocol actions (plan lines,
search queries, answers) and free-form reasoning text. Reward pressure
that leaks into the free-form channel inflates it: under a uniform KL
coefficient, every one of our six early training runs collapsed through
runaway growth of free-form tokens, and five successive interventions on
the gradient channel---token pricing at multiple rates, full and
asymmetric gradient masking---left the geometric growth rate essentially
unchanged (full forensics in Appendix~B). We therefore regularize the
two channels separately: a weak KL coefficient on action tokens, which
leaves the policy room to learn search behavior, and a strong KL
coefficient on free-form tokens, anchored to the SFT reference policy,
which rarely emits free-form reasoning. With the split coefficients in
place, inflation stopped and training ran cleanly for the full schedule.
A controlled comparison further shows that the anchor's \emph{identity}
carries the effect: re-anchoring the reference from the SFT policy to a
mid-training checkpoint that reasons freely re-ignited inflation within
seven episodes (mean free-form tokens $0.7 \rightarrow 7.2$), while an
otherwise-identical run that kept the SFT anchor trained without
incident.

\subsection{Training procedure}
\label{sec:method-algo}

Each training step proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Sample $K$ rollouts per question from the current policy under the
  search protocol.
\item Parse actions; execute searches against the environment; track
  first-time gold-title hits per rollout.
\item Compute trajectory returns by Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}, applying the
  completion gate and cost terms.
\item Compute leave-one-out advantages within each question group and
  broadcast each trajectory's advantage to its tokens.
\item Apply the policy-gradient update with channel-specific KL
  regularization; a small auxiliary value head serves as a variance
  monitor (Appendix~C).
\end{enumerate}
Hyperparameters, infrastructure, and guard criteria are given in
Appendix~C.
