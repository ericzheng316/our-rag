\section{Why Does Coverage Help?}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Section~\ref{sec:results} measures the effect of coverage shaping on
deep-hop accuracy; this section audits what the term changes about the
groups the estimator learns from.

\subsection{Audit protocol}
\label{sec:mech-protocol}

We sample $K{=}8$ rollouts per question at the training temperature
from the SFT initialization on a stratified closed-pool question set
($n{=}160/320/319$ complete groups at 2/3/4 hops), compute each
rollout's coverage by the reward's definition (\S\ref{sec:method}), and
classify every group by its answer outcomes and its within-group
coverage variation. The same audit is repeated on the trained policy to
track how the ledger evolves (\S\ref{sec:mech-evolution}).

\subsection{Coverage supplies ordering where outcomes are silent}
\label{sec:mech-repair}

Table~\ref{tab:repair} extends the tie ledger of
Table~\ref{tab:tie-ledger} with the repair. Under outcome-only reward,
a group is informative exactly when it is mixed: 29--44\% of groups,
and only 38.2\% at four hops. Counting a group as informative when
either the answer reward or coverage discriminates, the share at
four hops rises to 93.4\%---a $\times$2.4 expansion, the largest of any
depth. The raw material behind this is the variation documented in
\S\ref{sec:starvation}: within answer-tied groups, the share whose
rollouts differ in coverage rises from 38.9\% (2-hop) through 64.8\% to
89.3\% at four hops. Deep tied groups are not uniform failures; they
are failures that differ in how much of the evidence chain they
recovered, and coverage converts exactly that difference into
preference.

\subsection{Coverage measures progress, not correctness}
\label{sec:mech-not}

Coverage is a weak predictor of answer correctness. Within mixed
groups, where the question is defined, tie-aware within-group AUC of
coverage against correctness is 0.62/0.59/0.56 at 2/3/4 hops at the
SFT initialization, and lower after training.\footnote{Average-rank tie
handling; coverage values are coarse.} Reranking sampled rollouts by
coverage at inference time yields no reliable gain
(\S\ref{sec:placement}). What coverage measures is \emph{progress
through the annotated evidence chain}; its effect as a training signal
is the matched comparison of \S\ref{sec:results}. Across questions,
coverage stratifies risk (\S\ref{sec:placement}).

\subsection{Ties persist throughout training}
\label{sec:mech-evolution}

Auditing the trained policy, answer-tied groups remain the majority at
every depth ([55.2]\% at four hops, [42.6]\% uniformly wrong), and the
informative-share expansion from coverage remains
[$\times$2.1].\footnote{[K6-partial] Trained-policy numbers currently
use 6 of 8 sampling channels; final numbers will use all 8.} Answer
ties are a standing property of deep search under terminal rewards,
not a transient of initialization.

\subsection{The mechanism, end to end}
\label{sec:mech-chain}

The audit closes the loop opened in \S\ref{sec:starvation}:
(i)~deep-hop groups are predominantly answer-tied, and with depth their
ties shift from all-right to all-wrong;
(ii)~inside those tied groups, evidence progress still varies---at four
hops, almost always;
(iii)~coverage converts that variation into within-group preferences,
expanding the share of groups that provide any ordering by
$\times$2.4 at four hops, where all-wrong ties are most common; and
(iv)~the matched ablation (\S\ref{sec:results}) shows these preferences
causally improve deep-hop accuracy without increasing search. Terminal
rewards fail deep questions as a matter of arithmetic; evidence
coverage repairs the arithmetic where it fails.
