\section{Why Does Coverage Help?}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The ablation in \S\ref{sec:results} establishes \emph{that} coverage
shaping improves deep-hop accuracy; this section asks \emph{why}, by
auditing what the term changes about the groups the estimator learns
from.

\subsection{Audit protocol}
\label{sec:mech-protocol}

We sample $K{=}8$ rollouts per question at the training temperature
from the SFT initialization on a stratified closed-pool question set
($n{=}160/320/319$ complete groups at 2/3/4 hops), compute each
rollout's coverage exactly as the reward does (\S\ref{sec:method}), and
classify every group by its terminal outcomes and its within-group
coverage variation. The same audit is repeated on the trained policy to
track how the ledger evolves (\S\ref{sec:mech-evolution}).

\subsection{Coverage supplies ordering where outcomes are silent}
\label{sec:mech-repair}

Table~\ref{tab:repair} extends the tie ledger of
Table~\ref{tab:tie-ledger} with the repair. Under outcome-only reward,
a group is informative exactly when it is mixed: 29--44\% of groups,
and only 38.2\% at four hops. Counting a group as informative when
either terminal correctness or coverage discriminates, the share at
four hops rises to 93.4\%---a $\times$2.4 expansion, the largest of any
depth. The raw material behind this is the variation documented in
\S\ref{sec:starvation}: within answer-tied groups, the share whose
rollouts differ in coverage rises from 38.9\% (2-hop) through 64.8\% to
89.3\% at four hops. Deep tied groups are not uniform failures; they
are failures that differ in how much of the evidence chain they
recovered, and coverage converts exactly that difference into
preference.

\subsection{What coverage is not: a correctness predictor}
\label{sec:mech-not}

It is tempting to read coverage as a proxy for answer correctness. The
data reject this reading, and the distinction matters. Within mixed
groups---the only place the question is even defined---ranking rollouts
by coverage barely identifies the correct ones: tie-aware within-group
AUC is 0.62/0.59/0.56 at 2/3/4 hops at the SFT initialization, and
lower still after training.\footnote{Computed with average-rank tie
handling; coverage values are coarse, and naive rank implementations
inflate these numbers substantially.} Coverage is weakly informative
about \emph{who answers correctly}, and this is consistent with our
finding that coverage-based reranking of rollouts at inference time
yields no reliable gain (\S\ref{sec:placement}). What coverage measures
is \emph{progress through the annotated evidence chain}---a quantity
that is meaningful as supervision precisely because it comes from gold
annotations rather than from a prediction of correctness. Its
legitimate validation is therefore not predictive power but the causal
ablation of \S\ref{sec:results}: ordering failed rollouts by evidence
progress trains a better deep-hop policy. (Coverage does stratify risk
usefully \emph{across} questions; that distinct, diagnostic use is
taken up in \S\ref{sec:placement}.)

\subsection{Starvation persists throughout training}
\label{sec:mech-evolution}

One might hope ties are a cold-start artifact that training dissolves.
They are not: auditing the trained policy, answer-tied groups remain
the majority at every depth ([55.2]\% at four hops, [42.6]\% uniformly
wrong), and the informative-share expansion from coverage remains
[$\times$2.1].\footnote{[K6-partial] Trained-policy numbers currently
use 6 of 8 sampling channels; final numbers will use all 8.} The
starvation that coverage repairs is a standing property of deep search
under terminal rewards, not a transient of initialization---the repair
pays throughout the schedule.

\subsection{The mechanism, end to end}
\label{sec:mech-chain}

The audit closes the loop opened in \S\ref{sec:starvation}:
(i)~deep-hop groups are predominantly answer-tied, and with depth their
ties shift from benign to uniformly wrong;
(ii)~inside those tied groups, evidence progress still varies---at four
hops, almost always;
(iii)~coverage converts that variation into within-group preferences,
expanding the share of groups that teach anything by $\times$2.4 where
starvation is worst; and
(iv)~the matched ablation (\S\ref{sec:results}) shows these preferences
causally improve deep-hop accuracy without increasing search. Terminal
rewards fail deep questions as a matter of arithmetic; evidence
coverage repairs the arithmetic where it fails.
