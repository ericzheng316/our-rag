\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multi-hop question answering requires an agent to acquire evidence
sequentially: each retrieval exposes an intermediate entity that the next
query depends on. Reinforcement learning has recently become the standard
recipe for training such search agents: the policy interleaves reasoning,
search, and answering over several turns, and is optimized with a
group-relative estimator---GRPO or leave-one-out
REINFORCE~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath,ahmadian2024rloo,kool2019buy}---against
a terminal reward for answer correctness~\citep{jin2025searchr1,
chen2025research,song2025r1searcher,sun2025zerosearch}. These recipes
improve aggregate accuracy, but their gains concentrate on shallow
questions; on the deepest chains, where sequential evidence acquisition
matters most, improvements remain modest.

The standard diagnosis for this is temporal: rewards are sparse along the
trajectory, so intermediate progress goes uncredited. We argue for a
different, \emph{group-level} diagnosis. A group-relative estimator learns
only from comparisons among the $K$ rollouts of the same question---and on
hard questions, the terminal signal routinely fails to differ. This
degenerate regime is known in mathematical RLVR, where uniform-outcome
groups are discarded by dynamic sampling~\citep{yu2025dapo,2025greso} or
patched with synthetic advantages~\citep{hu2026avspo}. In deep search we
find it is not an occasional efficiency loss but the \emph{dominant}
regime, and it worsens structurally with depth: on MuSiQue 4-hop
questions, [84]\% of rollout groups are \emph{answer-tied}---every rollout
wrong (or, rarely, every rollout right).\footnote{Measured on stratified
closed-pool rollouts; see \S\ref{sec:mechanism} for the protocol.
Percentages here are [K8-pending] preliminary values.} In an answer-tied
group the terminal reward provides no within-group ordering whatsoever: it
cannot prefer a rollout that recovered half of the required evidence chain
over one that found nothing. What survives is worse than silence. The
residual ranking signal inside an all-failure group comes from the cost
and penalty terms---search cost, token cost, format penalties---which
order trajectories by how \emph{cheaply} they failed. On precisely the
questions where the agent most needs to learn persistence, the effective
preference points toward giving up early. Discarding these groups wastes
the hardest questions; filling them with content-free signal leaves their
internal ordering arbitrary---and recent evidence shows that whatever
signal occupies an all-failure group can come to govern the learned policy
outright~\citep{wang2026darkroom}. What tied groups need is an ordering
that \emph{means} something.

Standard multi-hop training sets already contain the information that
breaks these ties: gold supporting documents~\citep{trivedi2022musique,
yang2018hotpotqa}. We add to the trajectory return a
\emph{completion-gated, first-hit evidence-coverage} term: a rollout is
credited once per gold document it retrieves, normalized by the size of the
gold set, and receives this credit only if it commits to an answer. The
term is deterministic, judge-free, and computed from training annotations
alone---nothing is required at test time. Under the trajectory-level
group-relative estimator we use, this term does not perform step-level
credit assignment~\citep{lightman2024verify,uesato2022solving}; its effect
is simpler and, we argue, the one that matters: it \emph{restores
within-group ranking}. Uniformly failing rollouts become ordered by how
much of the required evidence they actually reached, rather than by how
little they spent failing.

Our experiments on MuSiQue with a Qwen3.5-9B policy support four findings.
\textbf{First}, coverage shaping delivers consistent deep-hop gains: across
explicit-seed training runs, 4-hop exact match improves by
[+2.7 to +7.2] points over an outcome-only counterpart trained
identically, while overall EM moves in the same direction in every
run---and 4-hop search counts do not increase, so the gain is not bought
with a larger search budget. \textbf{Second}, a group-level audit explains
where the gain comes from: adding coverage restores ranking information in
[31--46]\% of all-failure groups, roughly tripling the fraction of 4-hop
groups from which the estimator can learn anything at all---exactly where
the terminal signal is most starved. \textbf{Third}, the benefit has a
boundary, and the boundary is informative: gains transfer zero-shot to
2WikiMultihopQA, whose questions share the deep compositional structure the
reward targets, but vanish on open-corpus MuSiQue, where the bottleneck is
whether the evidence can be \emph{reached} by retrieval at all rather than
whether reached evidence is preferred. Evidence-coverage rewards fix a
discrimination problem; they cannot fix a reachability problem.
\textbf{Fourth}, placement matters: the same evidence signal that works as
a training reward and as an offline diagnostic produces no reliable
improvement when used as a runtime controller or injected as an
observation, under matched controls. Evidence supervision is
placement-sensitive: a good training signal and a useful audit, but not a
runtime advisor.

Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
\item \textbf{Answer-signal starvation in deep search, quantified.} While
  uniform-outcome groups are a known inefficiency in mathematical
  RLVR~\citep{yu2025dapo,hu2026avspo}, we show that in multi-hop search
  they are the dominant regime, scale structurally with hop depth
  ([75/71/84]\% at 2/3/4 hops), and carry a perverse residual ordering:
  inside all-failure groups the surviving preference is cost-driven,
  favoring cheap early abandonment (\S\ref{sec:starvation}).
\item \textbf{A trajectory-ranking repair.} Completion-gated first-hit
  coverage---deterministic, judge-free, training-time-only---restores
  within-group preferences among uniformly failing rollouts, without
  claiming step-level credit assignment (\S\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{Seed-consistent deep-hop gains without search inflation, and
  their boundary.} 4-hop improvements are consistent across explicit seeds
  and do not increase search calls; transfer succeeds where compositional
  structure matches and stops where retrieval reachability binds
  (\S\ref{sec:results}, \S\ref{sec:transfer}).
\item \textbf{A signal-placement study.} Under matched controls, evidence
  signals help as training rewards and offline diagnostics but not as
  runtime controllers or injected observations---negative results we
  report with their pre-registered stopping rules (\S\ref{sec:placement}).
\end{itemize}
