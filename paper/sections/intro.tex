\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

  Multi-hop question answering requires an agent to acquire evidence
  sequentially: each retrieval exposes an intermediate entity that the next
  query depends on. Reinforcement learning has become the standard way to
  train such search agents---the policy interleaves reasoning, search, and
  answering over several turns, optimized with a group-relative estimator
  such as GRPO or leave-one-out
  REINFORCE~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath,ahmadian2024rloo,kool2019buy}
  against a terminal reward for answer
  correctness~\citep{jin2025searchr1,chen2025research,song2025r1searcher,
  sun2026zerosearch}. This recipe improves aggregate accuracy, but its gains
  concentrate on shallow questions; on the deepest chains, where sequential
  evidence acquisition matters most, improvements remain modest.

  The standard diagnosis is temporal: rewards are sparse along the
  trajectory, so intermediate progress goes uncredited. We argue for a
  different, \emph{group-level} diagnosis. A group-relative estimator learns
  only from comparisons among the $K$ rollouts of the same question---and on
  hard questions, the terminal signal routinely fails to differ. In deep
  search this degenerate regime is not an occasional efficiency loss but the
  dominant case: at the initialization training starts from, 56--71\% of
  rollout groups are \emph{answer-tied} across depths. Depth changes the
  ties' character more than their count---at two hops many ties are
  benign, every rollout right, while on 4-hop questions benign ties all
  but vanish and 57\% of all groups are uniformly
  wrong.\footnote{Measured at the SFT initialization with $K{=}8$ rollouts
  at the training temperature; protocol in \S\ref{sec:mechanism}.} In an
  answer-tied
  group the terminal reward provides no within-group ordering whatsoever: it
  cannot prefer a rollout that recovered half of the required evidence chain
  over one that found nothing. What survives is worse than silence. The
  residual ranking signal inside an all-failure group comes from the cost
  and penalty terms---search cost, token cost, format penalties---which
  order trajectories by how \emph{cheaply} they failed. On precisely the
  questions where the agent most needs to learn persistence, the effective
  preference points toward giving up early.

  This failure mode has recently been named in mathematical
  RLVR---\emph{advantage collapse}~\citep{he2026collapse}---and existing
  mitigations fall into three families. Discarding methods drop or skip
  uniform-outcome groups by dynamic sampling or
  filtering~\citep{yu2025dapo,greso2025,xu2026downsampling}, recovering
  compute but abandoning exactly the hardest questions. Synthetic-advantage
  methods inject virtual reward samples so that collapsed groups still
  produce a learning signal~\citep{he2026collapse}, but the signal is
  content-free: the ordering inside a tied group remains arbitrary.
  Structural methods re-baseline the comparison itself, stratifying
  advantages by trajectory structure~\citep{zhu2025grpo} or comparing
  along shared tree prefixes~\citep{Ji2026tree}; this repairs
  cross-question fairness but leaves a fully tied group as uninformative as
  before. Leaving such groups to chance is not neutral: in GRPO training,
  whatever residual signal occupies an all-failure group can come to govern
  the learned policy outright~\citep{wang2026darkroom}. Tied groups should
  be neither thrown away nor filled with noise; what they need is an
  ordering that \emph{means} something.

  Standard multi-hop training sets already contain the information that
  breaks these ties: gold supporting documents~\citep{trived2022musique,
  yang2018hotpotqa}. We add to the trajectory return a
  \emph{completion-gated, first-hit evidence-coverage} term: a rollout is
  credited once for each gold document it retrieves (matched by title),
  normalized by the size of the gold set, and receives this credit only if
  it commits to an answer. The signal is deterministic and used only
  during training. Under a
  trajectory-level group-relative estimator the term acts on
  whole-trajectory returns; its effect---simpler than step-level process
  supervision~\citep{lightman2024verify,uesato2022solving}---is to
  \emph{restore within-group ranking}. Uniformly failing rollouts become ordered by how
  much of the required evidence they actually reached, rather than by how
  little they spent failing.

  Our experiments on MuSiQue with a Qwen3.5-9B policy yield four findings.
  \textbf{First}, coverage shaping delivers consistent deep-hop gains:
  across explicit-seed training runs, 4-hop exact match improves by
  [+2.7 to +7.2] points over an outcome-only counterpart trained
  identically, overall EM moves in the same direction in every run, and
  4-hop search counts do not increase. \textbf{Second}, a group-level
  audit locates the gain:
  at the training start, 89\% of 4-hop answer-tied groups carry coverage
  variation the terminal reward cannot see, and adding coverage expands
  the share of 4-hop groups with informative within-group ordering from
  38\% to 93\%---exactly where the terminal signal is most starved. \textbf{Third}, the benefit has an informative
  boundary: gains transfer zero-shot to 2WikiMultihopQA, whose questions
  share the deep compositional structure the reward targets, stay small on
  predominantly two-hop HotpotQA, and vanish on open-corpus MuSiQue, where
  the bottleneck is whether evidence can be \emph{reached} by retrieval at
  all rather than whether reached evidence is preferred. Evidence-coverage
  rewards fix a discrimination problem; they cannot fix a reachability
  problem. \textbf{Fourth}, the same signal that helps as a training reward
  and as an offline audit yields no reliable gain as a runtime controller
  or as an injected observation under matched controls: evidence
  supervision is placement-sensitive.

  Our contributions:
  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
  \item \textbf{A group-level diagnosis.} Answer-tied groups dominate
    deep-search RL (56--71\% of groups), shift with depth from benign
    all-correct ties to starved all-wrong ties (57\% of 4-hop groups),
    and carry a perverse cost-driven residual ordering---an account of
    deep-hop stagnation that
    temporal sparsity does not capture, complementing advantage-collapse
    analyses in mathematical RLVR~\citep{yu2025dapo,he2026collapse}
    (\S\ref{sec:starvation}).
  \item \textbf{A trajectory-ranking repair.} Completion-gated first-hit
    coverage---deterministic, judge-free, training-time-only---restores
    meaningful within-group preferences where prior mitigations discard,
    synthesize, or re-baseline (\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{Deep-hop gains with a mapped boundary.} Consistent 4-hop
    improvements across explicit seeds without search inflation; transfer
    governed by compositional match and retrieval reachability
    (\S\ref{sec:results}, \S\ref{sec:transfer}).
  \item \textbf{A signal-placement study.} The same evidence signal helps
    in training and offline audit but not as runtime control or injected
    observation (\S\ref{sec:placement}).
  \end{itemize}
